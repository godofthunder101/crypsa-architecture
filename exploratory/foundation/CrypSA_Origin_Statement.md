## Origin

CrypSA (Cryptid Server Architecture) was created by Beau Wells as a conceptual architecture for persistent digital universes.

The model emerged from an effort to rethink the foundations of multiplayer world design. Instead of treating online worlds as continuously simulated server state, CrypSA treats them as canonical structures that evolve through **validated canonical events** and are reconstructed locally by observers.

---

## Core Ideas (Early Formulation)

The original conceptual model centered on:

* minted canonical identities
* deterministic structural genomes
* invariant-protected canonical event history
* validated event-driven evolution
* observer-relative simulation
* lens-based interpretation layers

These ideas established the foundation for a system in which:

> shared reality is defined by canonical event history, not continuously synchronized state

---

## Architectural Direction

CrypSA was developed as an open architectural model to explore a different approach to online worlds:

* persistence independent of specific infrastructure
* replayable and reconstructable universes
* reduced reliance on centralized simulation
* clear separation between structural reality and observer experience

Rather than binding a universe to a specific runtime system, CrypSA explores how a universe can be defined in a way that allows it to be:

* preserved
* reconstructed
* interpreted across different systems

---

## Evolution of the Model

Since its initial formulation, CrypSA has evolved into a structured architecture based on four responsibilities:

* **Truth** — validation and canonical events
* **Translation** — adapters
* **Interpretation** — lenses
* **Experience** — observer simulation and UI

The concept of a **validator** has replaced the earlier implicit notion of a centralized server, clarifying that:

> authority is a role, not a location
